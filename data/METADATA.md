# Dataset provenance and limitations

**File:** `data/ev_market_dataset.csv` (1,263 rows) · **Regenerate:** `python scripts/generate_dataset.py` · **Seed:** 42, fully reproducible.

## What this is

A modelled EV market dataset covering 119 vehicle lines, 52 brands, 11 manufacturing
countries and 5 battery chemistries across model years 2018-2025.

**The vehicle identities are real. The numbers are modelled, not measured.**

Model names, body segments, countries of origin and battery chemistries correspond to
actual production vehicles — the Tesla Model 3 really is built in China and the US, BYD
really does use LFP Blade cells, and the NIO ET7 really did offer a 150 kWh semi-solid-state
pack. Grid carbon intensity per country is taken from published national estimates.

Prices, driving ranges, annual sales volumes, customer ratings and emissions figures are
**generated by the rules in `scripts/generate_dataset.py`**. No figure in this dataset should
be quoted as an observed market statistic.

This dataset is used instead of a Kaggle download because the closest-matching public dataset
([EV Market Analytics 2026](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/patelris/electric-vehicle-market-and-pricing-dataset-2026))
is also synthetic, carries no battery-chemistry column, and builds range as a single global
constant times capacity (`range = capacity x 3.5`), which would make the capacity-versus-range
chart a straight line rather than an analysis.

## How derived fields are computed

| Field | Derivation |
|---|---|
| `driving_range_miles` | `capacity / energy_consumption`, using a per-model Wh-per-mile figure that varies by body type and trim |
| `energy_consumption_wh_per_mi` | Model baseline, improved ~0.6%/yr, raised for Performance trims |
| `charging_time_min_10_80` | `0.70 x capacity / peak_kw x 60 x 1.18` |
| `price_usd` | Model baseline with ~1.2%/yr deflation to year 4, then ~5.5%/yr for the price war |
| `annual_sales_units` | Adoption curve from launch to peak year, decayed after peak, scaled by a market-size index, split across trims by share |
| `customer_rating` | Composite of range, price-per-range value, safety, charging speed and model age, rank-mapped onto 3.05-4.85 |
| `estimated_manufacturing_co2e_tonnes` | Body-type base + `capacity x 0.066 x chemistry_factor x grid_factor` |
| `estimated_annual_co2e_saved_tonnes` | ICE tailpipe baseline for that body type minus grid-based charging emissions, over body-typical annual mileage |

Range is deliberately **not** a constant multiple of capacity. Efficiency varies by body type,
so pooled capacity-vs-range correlation is 0.37 while correlation within a single body type is
0.85-0.95. Colouring that scatter by category shows three distinct bands instead of one line.

## Caveats that affect interpretation

**Growth statistics are partly a coverage artifact.** The catalogue contains far more models in
2025 than in 2018 because most listed vehicles launched after 2018. Absolute year-on-year growth
per category therefore mixes real market growth with growing catalogue coverage. Category
comparisons in `Q4` are expressed as a **share of each year's volume**, which is robust to this;
do not quote per-category absolute growth.

**All share figures are share of dataset, not share of market.** Total modelled volume is
24.1M units across 2018-2025. Real global EV sales over the same span were roughly three times
larger, because this catalogue covers the leading models, not the whole market. Any "market
share" label on a dashboard must read **"share of dataset sales"**.

**Grid intensity is applied to the manufacturing country as a proxy** for both cell production
and vehicle use. In reality a China-built car is usually charged in its sales market, not where
it was made. The per-unit CO2e figures are directionally useful and geographically imprecise.

**`acceleration_0_60_mph` is not meaningful for buses and heavy trucks** and is retained only for
schema consistency. Commercial-vehicle `customer_rating` represents fleet-operator satisfaction.

**Semi-solid-state and sodium-ion rows are very few** (8 and 4 rows, 0.62% of 2025 volume).
Neither chemistry had meaningful production scale in the modelled window, which is itself a
finding — but any chart averaging over them is statistically thin and should be labelled as such.

## Headline results from the SQL layer

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| Market growth | 284% YoY (2019) declining to 8.6% (2025). Growth is maturing, not stalling. |
| Volume leader | Tesla leads 2018-2023, BYD takes over in 2024. Top-5 share falls from 84% to 47%. |
| Brand count | 9 active brands in 2018 to 51 in 2025. The market is fragmenting. |
| Category shift | SUV share of yearly volume 32.5% to 55.4%; hatchbacks 38.8% to 19.8%. |
| Chemistry | NMC 51.0% of units but 56.4% of GWh; LFP 47.7% of units but 41.6% of GWh. LFP wins on vehicles, NMC on energy. |
| Price and range | Weighted average price falls $48,407 to $33,376 while weighted range rises 250 to 283 miles. |
| Grid effect | Same-size EV avoids 2.54 t/yr in Sweden, 1.35 t/yr in China, 1.23 t/yr in Vietnam. |
| Manufacturing payback | 2.7 years (LFP, France) to 6.5 years (NMC, Czechia). |
| Emerging tech | Solid-state passenger cars reach 402 miles vs 302 for lithium-ion, on 0.6% of volume, at the highest cell footprint (21.3 t). |
